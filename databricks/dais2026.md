# Databricks Data + AI Summit 2026 — Research Overview

> **Purpose:** Factual basis for a presentation on the major subjects and highlights of DAIS 2026.
> **Method:** Multi-agent deep research (101 agents) over the official agenda, Databricks newsroom/blogs, tech press, and social. 80 claims extracted, 25 adversarially fact-checked (3-vote), 23 confirmed, 2 refuted.
> **Compiled:** 2026-06-19. The summit ran June 15–18, 2026, so some product statuses (Beta/Preview) reflect announcement-time labels.
> **Confidence legend:** 🟢 High (multiple independent sources) · 🟡 Medium (single/primary-only or vendor-reported) · 🔴 Refuted (do **not** use).

---

## TL;DR — The Story to Tell

DAIS 2026 was, top to bottom, about **agentic AI**. The official tagline — **"Build apps and agents that work"** — set the frame, and CEO Ali Ghodsi's keynote sharpened it into the summit's signature claim: **"AGI is already here."** His argument: AI doesn't have an *intelligence* problem, it has a *context and deployment* problem. The job now is wiring agents into the enterprise as autonomous "workers."

Three product narratives carried that theme:

1. **Agent Bricks** — the agent-building platform, now with broad model/framework support and integrated governance.
2. **Unity AI Gateway + Unity Catalog** — governance extended from data to *agents, tools, and models*.
3. **LTAP + Lakehouse//RT** — a real-time architecture pitched as "the end of pipelines," because agents need live data.

## Biggest Highlights

### 🔑 Narrative highlight: "AGI is already here" 🟢
Ghodsi's keynote reframed the AI conversation:
> *"We believe that AGI is already here… The problem is that AGI is not completely permeating our organizations."* — Ali Ghodsi (via SiliconANGLE)

AI has a **context/deployment problem, not an intelligence problem.** Agents should be treated as autonomous enterprise workers. This was the single most-discussed takeaway across press (TIME: *"Databricks' CEO Believes AGI Is Already Here"*; BigDATAwire: *"AI Doesn't Have an Intelligence Problem, It Has a Context Problem"*).

### 🔧 Product highlight 1 — Agent Bricks (expanded) 🟢/🟡
- Expanded into a comprehensive **developer agent platform with integrated governance**.
- **Vendor metrics 🟡 (self-reported, not independently audited):** 100,000+ agents built since launch; processing **1+ quadrillion tokens/year**.
- **Model providers:** OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Qwen, **Kimi** (new), and **Grok** (new, *natively available via a SpaceX partnership*).
- **Frameworks:** LangGraph, Agno, CrewAI, **Claude Code SDK**, OpenAI Agent SDKs.
- **Plus:** Lakebase agent memory and a Databricks Sandbox.
- ℹ️ Note: Agent Bricks was *first launched at DAIS 2025* and **expanded** (not introduced) in 2026.

### 🔧 Product highlight 2 — Unity AI Gateway & Unity Catalog governance 🟢
- **Unity AI Gateway:** unified governance for models, tools, MCPs, agents, and external agents.
  - **Contextual Service Policies** (Beta)
  - Spend controls / budgets across external providers, including **bring-your-own-key**
  - **Unified agent tracing** in one governed telemetry layer
  - **Smart Routing** for model selection
- **Unity Catalog** now registers agents, tools, and models alongside data.
- **Governance Hub** (Private Preview): centralized command center for governance posture across data, AI, cost, and performance.

> ⚠️ **Do not overstate GA status.** Verification refuted claims that these features are *generally available* — most are **Beta / Private Preview**. See [Refuted](#refuted-claims--do-not-use).

### 🔧 Product highlight 3 — LTAP & Lakehouse//RT ("end of pipelines") 🟢/🟡
- **LTAP (Lake Transactional/Analytical Processing):** unifies transactions, analytics, streaming, and operational data on a **single copy of storage** in the lake — *"No pipelines synchronizing operational and analytical stores."*
  - Builds on **Lakebase** (serverless Postgres); writes transactional data directly into open columnar formats (**Delta Lake, Apache Iceberg**) while keeping PostgreSQL compatibility.
  - Motivation: AI agents need real-time data; *"the data stack becomes the bottleneck"* (VP Shanku Niyogi).
- **Lakehouse//RT:** real-time analytics engine (code-named **"Reyden" / "Reynold's Dream Engine"** after Reynold Xin).
  - **Vendor figures 🟡 (announced, not independently benchmarked):** latency as low as **10ms** (small datasets), **sub-100ms** at **12,000 queries/sec**; supports tens of thousands of concurrent users and agents.

### 🔧 Product highlight 4 — Genie One & Genie Ontology 🟢
- **Genie One:** an enhanced agentic *"data-smart AI coworker."*
- **Genie Ontology:** a live, self-improving context layer that learns from business data and uses a **PageRank-like** approach to weigh the authority/relevance of enterprise information (Ghodsi made the explicit Google PageRank analogy).

## Refuted Claims — DO NOT USE 🔴

These surfaced in coverage but **failed adversarial verification (0–3 votes).** Avoid them in the presentation:

1. ❌ **"Unity AI Gateway features were made generally available"** (unified spend visibility, granular cost attribution, hard spend caps, smart routing). → They are largely **Beta/preview**, not GA.
2. ❌ **"Unity Catalog governance + managed MCP services (Google Drive, Jira, Confluence, Slack, GitHub, SharePoint) shipped GA."** → GA scope was **not confirmed**.

## Confidence & Caveats Summary

- **Solid (🟢):** dates/venue/scale, tagline, track structure, keynote lineup, the "AGI is already here" framing, and the *existence* of the major announcements (Agent Bricks expansion, Unity AI Gateway, LTAP, Lakehouse//RT, Genie One/Ontology).
- **Vendor-reported, treat as marketing claims (🟡):** 100k+ agents / 1+ quadrillion tokens; Lakehouse//RT latency/QPS/concurrency numbers. State the source ("Databricks reports…") rather than as independent fact.
- **Status labels:** Beta / Private Preview labels are announcement-time; some may reach GA later.
- **Social sentiment:** the X/LinkedIn community-reaction dimension is represented mainly via TIME, tech press, and a Matei Zaharia tweet (confirming the Reyden/Reynold Xin naming) — **not** a systematic social-sentiment survey.
